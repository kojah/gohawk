## What it detects

These experimental, opt-in checks include a caller waiting for completion before
receiving a worker's result, when that worker must finish an unbuffered result
send before it can signal completion. Neither goroutine can advance.

The analysis composes cached, ordered summaries across direct helper calls and
closures. It resolves channel parameters, captures, and supported local heap
storage to exact channel identities. Diagnostics point at the wait and include
related locations for the worker's send, completion signal, and later receive.

Two additional checks join mutex and worker dependencies:

- `lock-and-join`: a caller holds a fresh `sync.Mutex` before launching a
  worker, then waits for completion before unlocking. The worker must acquire
  that exact mutex before signaling completion.
- `channel-lock-cycle`: the caller holds the mutex across an unbuffered send
  or receive, while the worker must acquire it before the matching operation.

Both require one worker, complete ordered effects, and exact local
resource identities. Early unlocks, early completion, and different mutexes
do not establish a cycle. Buffered communication, read/write mutexes,
alternative participants, and cancellation/select branches remain outside
these new proofs. Related locations identify the worker acquisition, its
required operation, and the caller's later unlock.

The worker may precede this sequence with adjacent lock/unlock pairs on
different fresh local mutexes. Shared mutexes, nested locking, early signals,
and releases of the parent's mutex are not discarded to manufacture a cycle.

Enable it with `gohawk -enable-checks=channelprotocol/blocked-operation ./...`.

### Current boundary

The proof covers one caller and one worker with a local result channel and
an agreed operation sequence. The worker sends a scalar result before signaling
completion; the caller waits for completion before receiving the result. A
buffer that accommodates the result breaks this cycle. Completion may be:

- Closing a distinct, locally allocated completion channel, directly or with
  `defer close(done)`.
- Calling `Done`, directly or deferred, on a fresh local `sync.WaitGroup` with
  exactly one `Add(1)` before the worker is launched. The caller uses `Wait`.

Direct helpers and completion-only deferred helpers or closures compose with
these operations. Defers execute at their own function's return, in reverse
registration order. A helper that defers completion and returns before the
send does not establish this cycle.
Deferred helpers may combine unlock, channel close, and group completion.
Their internal operation order is preserved, including through imported and
forwarding helpers; deferred acquisition or blocking communication is unknown.

Visible helpers containing only supported, non-panicking scalar arithmetic
may have an empty protocol-effect summary. Scalar signatures alone do not
establish that guarantee. Division, dynamic shifts, opaque calls and callbacks
remain unknown, even if their result is just an integer.

Acyclic branches compose only when their ordered effects agree at every merge
and return, including pending defers. The collector visits each block once;
it does not enumerate paths or retain conditional effects.

Divergent branches, loops, `select`, other deferred actions, recursion, extra goroutines,
opaque calls, escaping resources, and unresolved or mutable captures remain
unknown. Dynamic group counts, group copying/resetting, `WaitGroup.Go`, and
groups supplied by callers remain outside this proof.
Unknown means no diagnostic, not that the program is safe. Unavailable
dependency bodies need a complete imported concurrency fact; missing facts
remain unknown. The shared `concurrencyfacts` pass exports ordered effects
on direct parameters (including mutexes and WaitGroups). It preserves order
through transitive helpers. Embedded mutex fields can also bind through local
or imported methods, using bounded field paths and an existing exact caller
address. Pointer-field dereferences and whole-owner resets remain unknown;
mixed-cycle proofs still require a fresh local owner. Local allocation identities
and captured cells are not exported. This is not a model of arbitrary
protocols or general deadlock freedom.

### Analysis cost

There is no schedule enumeration or product of goroutine states. Each check's
caller question has a 2,000-step shared instruction/storage budget, and summaries hold
at most 32 synchronization operations, including pending defers. Completed
callee summaries are cached during the package pass; recursion and exhausted
budgets never establish a defect.
Input discovery still scans the package's SSA. These bounds deliberately trade
coverage for predictable work, rather than making exhaustive analysis cheap.

func waitBeforeReceive() {
  results, done := make(chan int), make(chan struct{})
  go func() {
    results <- 42
    close(done)
  }()
  <-done

}

#### Waiting for a worker blocked on its result

```go gohawk="W3siY2hlY2siOiJjaGFubmVscHJvdG9jb2wvYmxvY2tlZC1vcGVyYXRpb24iLCJtZXNzYWdlIjoiV2FpdEdyb3VwIHdhaXQgcHJldmVudHMgdGhlIHdvcmtlcidzIHByZWNlZGluZyBzZW5kIGZyb20gY29tcGxldGluZyIsInN0YXJ0TGluZSI6OCwic3RhcnRDb2x1bW4iOjIsImVuZExpbmUiOjgsImVuZENvbHVtbiI6MTR9XQ"
func waitGroupBeforeResult() {
  var group sync.WaitGroup
  results := make(chan int)
  group.Add(1)
  go func() {
    defer group.Done()
    results <- 42
  }()
  group.Wait()
  <-results
}
```
