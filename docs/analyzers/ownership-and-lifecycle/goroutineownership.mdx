## What it detects

Reports goroutines whose join handle or lifecycle owner is not honored on every return path.

### Checks

{/* gohawk:generated-checks:start */}
| Check | Kind | Tier | What it detects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <CheckIdentity name="unjoined" tier="core" /> | hazard | core | Reports goroutines with a recognizable join or lifecycle mechanism that is not honored on every return path. |
| <CheckIdentity name="detached" tier="experimental" /> | hazard | experimental | Reports goroutines without a recognizable join handle or lifecycle owner. |
{/* gohawk:generated-checks:end */}

## Why this is flagged

A goroutine with no clear owner can keep running after its caller returns, or
block forever trying to send a result nobody will receive. These leaks hold
memory and other resources, and they can make shutdown nondeterministic or hang
the program outright.

Further reading: [Go concurrency patterns: Pipelines and cancellation](https://go.dev/blog/pipelines),
[Go concurrency patterns: Context](https://go.dev/blog/context).

## How to fix it

Join the goroutine, transfer its lifecycle explicitly, or connect it to a bounded owner.
