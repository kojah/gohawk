## What it detects

This experimental, opt-in check reports a caller waiting for completion before
receiving a worker's result, when that worker must finish an unbuffered result
send before it can signal completion. Neither goroutine can advance.

The analysis composes cached, ordered summaries across direct helper calls and
closures. It resolves channel parameters, captures, and supported local heap
storage to exact channel identities. Diagnostics point at the wait and include
related locations for the worker's send, completion signal, and later receive.

Enable it with `gohawk -enable-checks=channelprotocol/blocked-operation ./...`.

### Current boundary

The initial proof covers one caller and one worker, two distinct channels
allocated in the caller, and straight-line operations: the worker sends a scalar
result then closes the completion channel; the caller receives completion then
the result. A buffer that accommodates the result breaks this cycle.

Branches, loops, `select`, deferred actions, recursion, extra goroutines, opaque
calls, escaping channels, and unresolved or mutable captures remain unknown.
Unknown means no diagnostic, not that the program is safe. Dependency bodies
that are unavailable cannot supply a summary; this version exports no new
cross-package facts. It does not yet model mutexes, WaitGroups, arbitrary channel
protocols, or general deadlock freedom.

### Analysis cost

There is no schedule enumeration or product of goroutine states. Each caller
question has a 2,000-step shared instruction/storage budget, and summaries hold
at most 32 channel operations. Completed callee summaries are cached during the
package pass; recursion and exhausted budgets never establish a defect.
Input discovery still scans the package's SSA. These bounds deliberately trade
coverage for predictable work, rather than making exhaustive analysis cheap.
